
Linith


1. Overview

Linith is a contest of motion and stillness between the Sun ☼ and the Moon ☾.
Across a 10×10 grid, each commands formations of Swans and Stones, shaping and confining space through balance and foresight.
Victory is achieved by bringing all opposing Swans to stillness through perfect encirclement.


2. Components

Board - A 10×10 grid of neutral grey tiles.

Players -

The Sun ☼

The Moon ☾

Pieces -

Swan - A white tile marked with ☼ or ☾; represents grace, motion, and growth.

Stone - A black tile; represents strength, boundary, and confinement.


3. Objective

To encircle and freeze all of your opponent’s Swans.
A player wins when every opposing Swan is encircled.


4. Setup

The board begins entirely grey.

The Sun ☼ places the first Swan on any grey tile.

The Moon ☾ places a Swan on any grey tile not adjacent (orthogonal or diagonal) to the Sun’s Swan.

The Moon ☾ then takes the first turn.

Turns alternate thereafter.


5. Turn Structure

On your turn, perform one of the following actions -

A. Place a Swan

Place a new Swan on a grey tile adjacent (orthogonal) to one of your existing Swans.

A Swan cannot be placed adjacent to an opponent’s Swan.

Each player can have a maximum of six Swans on the board.

Once both players have six Swans on the board, each turn has two actions.

B. Place a Stone

Place a Stone on any grey tile.

C. Move Swan(s)

Move one or more of your existing Swans one tile in any direction (orthogonal or diagonal).

A Swan cannot move into any of the eight tiles (orthogonal or diagonal) surrounding an opponent’s Swan that has no Stones in any of its own eight surrounding tiles.

D. Push Swan(s)

Push an enemy Swan away from your own Swan. The enemy Swan must be active and in an adjacent tile (orthogonal or diagonal).


When a Swan moves (or is pushed), all Stones unique to that Swan move with it, maintaining their relative positions.

Shared Stones do not move -

If a Stone is adjacent to both your moving Swan(s) and an unmoved friendly Swan, it remains with the unmoved Swan.

If a Stone is adjacent to an opponent’s Swan, it also remains in place.

Swans and Stones can only move into grey (empty) tiles.

Frozen Swans cannot move, spawn, or move Stones.


6. Encirclement and Stillness

A Swan or connected group of Swans is encircled when all eight surrounding tiles (orthogonal and diagonal)(known as silvers) are occupied by Stones, Enemy Swans, andor the board’s edge.

When encircled -

The affected Swan(s) become frozen — turned to stillness.

Frozen Swans remain on the board as white tiles but are inactive -

They cannot move, spawn, or move Stones.

They continue to occupy space, affecting placement and encirclement around them.

Encircling an opponent's Swan grants one extra action per Swan frozen.

If a player’s final active Swan becomes encircled, that player loses the game.


7. Victory and End Conditions

Victory - You win when your opponent’s final active Swan is encircled.

Draw - If both players’ final active Swans are encircled during the same turn, the game ends in a draw.
